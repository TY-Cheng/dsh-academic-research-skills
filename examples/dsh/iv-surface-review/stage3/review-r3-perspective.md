# Stage 3 — Reviewer 3 (Perspective)

**Recommendation:** Major Revision

## Summary

The paper's central move—replace “is it arbitrage-free?” with “which guarantee, enforced how?”—is valuable. The paper is less clear about what kind of review it is and how far its practical guidance can be trusted. These are framing and validation issues, not data issues.

## Strengths

- The guarantee question is a genuine reframing.
- The paper avoids a method-ranking conclusion.
- The practical decision guide is the most useful part for a desk.
- The limitations are explicit.

## Major issues

**P1. The review type and protocol need a box.** The paper calls itself “structured” but does not state whether it is systematic, scoping, narrative-integrative, or rapid. It is a structured/narrative review with a frozen corpus; say so, and state that it is not PRISMA-compliant and not registered. This prevents a reader from treating the 40/351 flow as a systematic-review audit.

**P2. The seven-dimension matrix is a proposal; the paper sometimes treats it as a result.** Table 3 and §4.5 read as if the matrix were derived from the corpus, but the dimensions are the author's synthesis. Keep “proposed” in the table caption, the abstract, and the conclusion; add a validation agenda (inter-rater coding, a pilot benchmark, or a Delphi panel).

**P3. The decision guide needs a use case and a warning.** Table 5 is useful, but a reader may apply it without checking the guarantee's assumptions. Add a short use case (e.g., a desk choosing between SVI and a neural generator) and a “check the guarantee, not the label” warning. The guide should not be read as a validated selection rule.

## Minor issues

- Add JEL codes if the venue expects them.
- Standardize “matrix” vs “framework” vs “taxonomy.”
- The Chinese abstract is a deliberate zh-CN deviation; disclose it in the process record.
- The abstract's “no method ranking” is good; keep one statement.

## What would make it acceptable

A review-type box, a consistently “proposed” matrix, and a use-case/warning for the decision guide. The paper would then be honest about what it is: a corpus-scoped, structured narrative review with a practical proposal.
