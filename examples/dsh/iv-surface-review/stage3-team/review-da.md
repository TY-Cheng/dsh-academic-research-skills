### Verdict and Recommendation (Advisory, Seat-Level)

**Verdict: MAJOR REVISION.** Not Accept, not Reject.

The core thesis survives the strongest counter-argument, and the disclosures are unusually honest; the paper is a usable map for a newcomer and a legitimate basis for a revised review. But six MAJOR issues block acceptance in the present form: the headline claims contradict the paper's own scoping (M1); the corpus and taxonomy coding are invalid as printed (M2, M4); the seven-dimension matrix has no source-level evidence behind it (M3); and the contribution's novelty and practitioner instrument are asserted rather than demonstrated (M5, M6). All are repairable inside the declared structured-review type without new data collection. If the corpus cannot be recoded, the paper should be repositioned as a commentary and the empirical claims dropped. The editorial synthesizer owns the final decision; this seat recommendation is not derived arithmetically from the criterion table.

### Genuine Strengths

- The central question — "which guarantee?" — is a genuinely useful reframing. It converts a binary that readers routinely conflate (arbitrage-free / not arbitrage-free) into a mechanism question with different failure modes, and §5.1's failure-mode mapping is the paper's best page.
- The static/dynamic distinction is stated correctly and without inflation: static admissibility is necessary but not sufficient, dynamic consistency is a joint condition rather than a shape condition, and the corpus lacks a shared dynamic test (§2.4).
- Methodological candour is exceptional for a review of this size: no full texts, no PRISMA compliance, ten preprints, four partial abstracts, one title-level record, HTTP 429 failures, and an uncoded 311-record screen are all disclosed rather than hidden (§§3.1–3.7, §5.4).
- Appendices B, D, and E together give the corpus an auditable source-level trail (DOIs, bucket, evidence level, enforcement coding, query counts) that most narrative reviews lack. The frozen-corpus artifact in `search/selected_sources.json` exists and is consistent with the paper's description.
- Table 6's propositions are stated with explicit falsifiers, and §5.2's "What would change this map" names genuine falsifiers. That is the right posture for a review's research agenda.
- The "What this review can and cannot support" paragraph (§5.5) is a model of claim bounding and should survive revision.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

If I held the opposite view, I would argue that this paper's headline contribution is a relabeling and its empirical content is too thin to license the claims it makes. That "arbitrage-free" covers hard parameter restrictions, soft penalties, two-step projections, and post-hoc repair is not a discovery: it is the standard taxonomy of constrained estimation, and the paper itself cites Homescu (2011) as already making "the constraint taxonomy explicit". The static/dynamic distinction is textbook no-arbitrage theory. What remains is a 40-abstract corpus, selected by arbitrage-flavoured query strings, coded by the author with no reliability check and no retained negative screen, from which the paper infers that dynamic work is under-tested and no shared benchmark exists — conclusions that follow from the search vocabulary as much as from the field. The seven-dimension matrix is a generic checklist; no source is shown to have been coded against it, so "common / uneven / rare" is impression, not evidence. The practical guide then abandons the enforcement axis for method-family columns and asserts family-level interpretability and cost orderings with no anchors — precisely the ranking the paper disclaims. On this reading the paper is a well-written vocabulary memo: useful for orienting a newcomer, but it does not change how a specialist classifies or evaluates methods, and its six limitations and repeated "corpus-scoped" caveats pre-empt the citable claims that would give it value. The "so what?" answer reduces to a call for a benchmark the field already knows it needs.

### What Is Missing

1. **A source-level coding matrix for the seven evaluation dimensions.** Table 4's "Reporting in corpus" column is the empirical basis for the review's second contribution and is not backed by any per-source data in the paper.
2. **A formal statement of each family's guarantee.** The paper never maps "construction / penalty / projection / generation-filter" onto the mathematical condition each does or does not satisfy (which parameter inequalities, what a projection must satisfy to be a guarantee, what a filter's acceptance test is). §5.2 mentions idempotence and stability of the repair map but never audits whether corpus repair rules satisfy them.
3. **A category-level comparison to the prior surveys it names.** Homescu (2011) and Ruf and Wang (2020) are invoked, not compared; without that, the novelty claim has no evidential content.
4. **A specification of the benchmark the paper calls for.** Table 4 lists dimensions and Table 8 lists questions; neither gives the minimum protocol (dataset, maturity/strike grid, violation tolerance, dynamic test statistic, reporting fields) that would let anyone build the benchmark.
5. **A record-level exclusion list** for the 311 non-advanced records, and a rationale file for the 40 inclusion decisions beyond the category-level note in Appendix C.
6. **Any engagement with negative or null results** in the corpus, despite §5.4 naming the absence of negative results as a limitation and §5.5 asking for them in future work.
7. **A full-text check of the ten preprints and four partial abstracts**, without which the taxonomy's mechanism labels are unverified at the source level.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Data-access economics rather than measurement neglect.** Reliable joint surface–underlying panels and licensed quote data are expensive and often proprietary; that single cause predicts both the thin dynamic testing and the fragmented, non-comparable benchmarks, and it implies a data-provision intervention rather than a reporting standard. It is more parsimonious than "the field measures what is easy to compute" because it explains why the easy measurements cluster where they do.
2. **Publication-incentive equilibrium.** Credit accrues to fit improvements and new architectures, not to violation audits, benchmark maintenance, or negative results; this explains not only the observed fragmentation but its persistence, and it predicts that a voluntary seven-dimension reporting standard would go unadopted — a prediction the paper's proposal does not confront.
3. **An evidence-hierarchy axis instead of a mechanism axis.** The corpus already carries VI/VII verification levels and a title-level record; organizing by evidence strength would answer the paper's own question ("how strong is the evidence for each family?") more directly, and would expose that roughly a quarter of the corpus is preprint- or abstract-level, without requiring a new taxonomy.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- Data vendors and exchanges that produce and license the surfaces.
- Benchmark maintainers and funders, i.e., whoever would own and sustain the proposed benchmark.
- Model-validation and internal-audit teams: named as the primary reader, but present in the corpus nowhere as evidence sources.
- Non-US and emerging-market practitioners and exchange venues, given the admitted SPX/US concentration.
- Authors of excluded records and negative results: the corpus contains no such voice.
- Supervisors and regulators: mentioned once (§5.3) while the paper explicitly declines to evaluate regulatory practice.

### Unexamined Premise

The paper assumes that the enforcement mechanism is the axis on which construction methods are, or should be, compared. If method choice is driven mainly by data availability, latency, software ecosystem, and vendor support, then a guarantee taxonomy answers a question practitioners are not asking — and the paper's own decision guide, which is keyed to method families, may be closer to the real decision than the taxonomy that precedes it. A related unexamined premise is that arbitrage-freeness is the dominant evaluation target; the paper never asks whether calibration stability or hedging performance might dominate for the stated reader. The review is a map keyed to a property the field may treat as a constraint rather than as an objective.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- The bilingual abstract and the full declaration block are above the norm for this kind of preprint.
- Appendix B, Appendix D, and Appendix E together provide a genuine source-level audit trail; this is an asset that Table 4 fails to use, not a flaw in the appendices.
- Table 6's propositions with explicit falsifiers are a good model for a research agenda; the problem is that §§5.2 and 5.5 then treat the propositions as if they answered the research question.
- Pre-disclosing the prior Devil's Advocate checkpoint and its five issues (§5.4) is honest practice; the panel should verify the fixes rather than presume them.
- The decision to decline PRISMA status explicitly, rather than imitate systematic-review rhetoric, is correct and should not be reversed in revision.
- The frozen-corpus artifact referenced in §3.7 exists on disk and matches the paper's description; this particular reproducibility claim checks out.
