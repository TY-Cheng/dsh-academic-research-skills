## Contract Paraphrase

D1 asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical presentation, and provision for replication are documented well enough for a competent reader outside the immediate subfield to judge them; as the outside-perspective seat I will read that record for transparency and followability rather than adjudicate the statistics myself.

D2 asks whether the manuscript's substantive claims about its own field agree with what that field currently accepts, whether the prior work it relies on is represented faithfully, and whether specialist vocabulary and reported results are used correctly; my contribution is limited to noticing where an adjacent-field reader would be misled by terminology or by an unstated domain convention.

D3 asks whether the central thesis holds together under its own evidence and whether it rests on unsupported premises, explicitly including premises about who gains, who pays, and who is excluded; from my angle I will treat stakeholder claims as part of argumentative support rather than as optional discussion.

D4 asks whether readers from neighbouring disciplines can enter the argument — whether the framing, definitions, and stated implications are legible to them, and whether claims that cross into other fields are supported rather than merely asserted; I will look for the specific points where an informed outsider would need translation, evidence, or a caveat.

D5 concerns the manuscript's organisation, clarity, figure and table quality, and conformity to the venue's presentation conventions; from outside the field I will judge whether the structure and signposting let a non-specialist locate the main claim and the evidence for it without specialist shortcuts.

D6 concerns fit with the configured outlet and the originality and importance of the contribution for that readership; my paraphrase keeps that question open until the manuscript is visible, because contribution cannot be settled from a title and a field label alone.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether adjacent-field readers can follow the paper's definitions, data conventions, and implications, and whether any interdisciplinary claim is argued from evidence rather than asserted.
what_triggers_block: Definitions or empirical conventions stay unusable outside the originating subfield while the paper's stated practical or interdisciplinary implications depend on that outside readership, with no bridging explanation.
what_triggers_warn: Some terms or empirical conventions need specialist knowledge and the practical or adjacent-field implications are stated without enough translation for readers outside the subfield.
