## Review Body

This is a disciplined, unusually self-aware structured review. The screening arithmetic reconciles, the four-locus framework is operationalized in Table 2 rather than merely asserted, the dynamic-void claim is explicitly bounded to 41 abstracts with two rival explanations left open, and the no-comparability rule is stated before the synthesis and applied to it. Those are real methodological achievements, and they make the manuscript's defects easier to locate rather than harder.

The defects are concentrated in the coding and selection record, in one hypothesis adjudication, and in one conclusion-level overclaim. The manuscript uses one definition for a numerical test and the same words for an audit row, yet reports 16 of the former and 6 of the latter; the proof–audit gap, a headline finding, inherits the discrepancy. All coding was done by one analyst at abstract level with no reliability check, which leaves the corpus-wide counts stable only as one reader's judgment. The search and screening path is arithmetically exact but not reconstructable: query strings are not reported, 40 discretionary exclusions are not itemized, and the two post-screening replacements are described differently in two places. Separately, Table 4 adjudicates H2 against a fit-error clause that the declared H2 does not contain. These are repairable, but they must be repaired before the counts and the hypothesis table can be read as stated. The central descriptive thesis — four enforcement loci licensing four evidence types, with dynamic enforcement absent from the corpus — survives them.

**Strengths**

The retrieval and screening counts reconcile exactly: 31 queries at 25 records each give 775 requested, one lost query leaves 750 returned, de-duplication removes 320 to leave 430 unique, the eight exclusion codes sum to 389, and 430 minus 389 is 41. Composition also reconciles: 36 in-window plus 5 seminal precursors, 29 peer-reviewed plus 11 preprints plus one practitioner article, and family sizes 8, 6, 9, 8, 5, 5 sum to 41. This is the kind of arithmetic a reader can check without the run directory, and it resists the common review failure in which the flow diagram does not add up.

**Weaknesses**

### W1: The audit-row count does not follow from the stated definition of a numerical test

§3.2 defines a numerical test as a record that reports "a residual or violation check on its own output." The Table 3 note defines an audit row with exactly the same words. Yet Table 3 reports 16 numerical-test rows — 6 in N, 4 in R, 3 in G, 1 in C, 2 in S — and only 6 audit rows: N2 and N6, R5 and R7, G5 and G6. If the two terms are coextensive, the audit count should be 16; if "audit" means the narrower class of rows that report a quantified violation rate at a stated tolerance, that narrower predicate needs its own definition, and the §5.1 sentence "among the six audit rows" needs to say so. As written, the proof–audit gap — 17 proof rows, 6 audit rows, one overlap — cannot be reproduced from the manuscript's own coding rules. This is the review's central quantitative claim about evidence channels, so the definitions must be separated and the count rerun.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "An audit row reports a residual or violation check on its own output; G6 is the only row that also reports a structural guarantee."
**Confidence**: 5 — compared the §3.2 definition with the Table 3 note and recounted every family column; the predicates are coextensive and the totals differ.

### W2: The corpus-wide counts rest on single-analyst abstract-level coding with no reliability check

All coding, grading, and adjudication were performed by one analyst, with no dual screening, adjudication step, or inter-coder agreement statistic (§6.4; Appendix C). The evidence is abstract-level: 34 full abstracts, 4 partial, and 3 title-only. The headline counts — 17 proof, 16 test, 8 assumption, 0 of 41 dynamic, 0 direct comparability, 6 audit rows — are therefore one coder's reading of short texts, and the manuscript itself concedes that a different coder could shift rows at family borders. The disclosure is candid, but disclosure does not supply reliability. A second coder on a stratified sample, or a coding manual with adjudicated examples for the six audit rows and the five boundary cases, would let a reader separate a corpus-wide pattern from coding judgment. Until then the absence claims are warn-level, not because they are wrong but because their stability is unmeasured.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "Coding was single-analyst, retrieval was English-only and OpenAlex-only"
**Confidence**: 4 — read §3.2, §6.4, and Appendix C; the single-analyst disclosure is explicit, and no per-row reliability evidence is reported.

### W3: The search and selection path cannot be rerun from the manuscript

The arithmetic is impeccable, but the selection path is not reconstructable. §3.1 reports 31 queries by group and count but not their strings; Appendix A states that the 40 "relevant but not selected" exclusions are discretionary and recorded per record rather than reconstructed; and two included records were replaced during abstract reading, with the replacements drawn from the relevant-but-not-selected pool. The two accounts of the replacements also conflict: §3.1 says the retrieved text showed no enforcement claim and that both replacements are named in Appendix A, while Appendix A says metadata resolution and abstract reconstruction failed and only that the replacements are recorded in the Stage 1 bibliography. A reader can check the subtraction from 775 to 41 but cannot audit the search or the borderline inclusion decisions. The manuscript should report the 31 query strings, itemize the 40 discretionary exclusions or their decision rule, and give one consistent reason for the two replacements. For a review whose method is its corpus, the omission is a material reproducibility gap.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.1 and Appendix A — expected the 31 query strings, a per-record disposition of the 40 discretionary exclusions, and a record of the two post-screening replacements; checked §3.1, Appendix A, Table 1, Appendix B
**Confidence**: 4 — checked §3.1 and Appendix A for query strings and per-record dispositions, and compared the two replacement narratives.

A reader cannot tell whether the three rows are a declared exception or whether the rule was applied loosely at retrieval and tightened at coding. State the exception in §3.1 and align the inclusion sentence with Table 1.

### W6: H2 is adjudicated against a fit-error clause that the declared hypothesis does not contain

§1.2 declares H2 as the claim that penalty and repair routes "may displace rather than remove the constraint burden, absorbing it into weights, tolerances, and repair steps." Table 4 adjudicates H2 as "burden-displacement clause supported; fit-error clause unsupported," and its basis is that no penalty or repair row reports a lower fit error than a constrained parametric baseline. No fit-error clause appears in the declared H2. The verdict therefore tests a hypothesis the manuscript did not state, and it does so by treating the absence of a cross-family fit comparison as evidence against a clause while §5.2 says such cross-family comparison is not licensed in the first place. This is not fatal: the displacement finding is independently supported by the weights, tolerances, and repair steps the review documents. But the table must either restore the declared H2 and drop the fit-error clause, or disclose a revised hypothesis and label the revision as post hoc. As written, a reader cannot tell whether H2 was supported or partly refuted.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "penalty and repair routes may displace rather than remove the constraint burden"
**Confidence**: 5 — compared the §1.2 H2 declaration with the Table 4 verdict and basis line by line.

### W7: The conclusion claims the review identifies what methods cost, while the findings say cost is only asserted

§7.2 tells practitioners that the review "identifies what each method's guarantee is worth and what it costs." §5.3 and Table 6 say the opposite about cost: computational cost is "asserted" in about five rows, "0 comparable," and no row reports wall-clock or memory on a fixed grid. The conclusion converts an asserted cost into an identified cost, which is the kind of claim inflation the review warns against elsewhere. The fix is small: say the review identifies what each guarantee licenses and what the corpus claims about cost, not what it costs. This does not affect the central findings, but it is a conclusion-level overclaim in the practical implication.
