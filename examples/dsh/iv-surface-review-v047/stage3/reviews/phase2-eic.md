## Review Body

This seat reads the editorial surface of the manuscript: whether the document is organised so an editor can follow the argument from title through conclusion, whether its exhibits are self-explanatory and correctly tied to their claims, and whether the abstract, body, and appendices say the same thing. Substantive statistical, domain, and cross-disciplinary claims belong to the other seats. D6 is assessed only on its contribution half, on a field-general basis: the report retains the manuscript's `criteria_binding_unavailable` status, and no venue, tier, or submission-readiness claim is made.

On that basis D6 passes. The article type is declared in the front matter and Section 1.2 — a structured, corpus-scoped review with a framework contribution — and the body is consistent with that framing rather than drifting into an empirical or systematic-review claim. The contribution (a 41-source evidence map, the four-locus framework, the proof/audit gap, and the bounded dynamic void) is original in framing and calibrated to its evidence; the manuscript does not rank methods, claim new empirics, or assert that the framework is validated. The unresolved production layer recorded under D5 is a presentation matter, not a contribution defect. The fit half of D6 remains unassessable under `criteria_binding_unavailable`; no venue-specific fit, tier, or readiness judgement is implied.

The manuscript is, on the whole, unusually well organised for a 41-source review. Its weaknesses are localised rather than structural, and they concentrate in the abstract and in the production layer rather than in the argument itself.

### S1: An explicit roadmap and a consistent section architecture

The introduction states the full section plan, and every findings section returns to the same locus–evidence–verification template, so a reader always knows which question a paragraph is answering. The four-family account is not allowed to drift into a narrative of methods.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: "Section 2 defines the object and the framework, Section 3 the corpus and evidence rules"
**Confidence**: 5 — the roadmap is explicit and is followed in Sections 2–7.

### S2: Transparent evidence boundaries and disclosed provenance

The manuscript repeatedly states what its evidence can and cannot establish: the review is "PRISMA 2020-style, not PRISMA-compliant", coding is single-analyst and abstract-level, no theorem was checked, and no reported number was recomputed. Appendix A records the retrieval arithmetic, Appendix B the per-row ledger, and Appendix C the coding protocol and boundary cases. The hypotheses are declared before the findings and adjudicated with residual uncertainty in Table 4.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: "PRISMA 2020-style, not PRISMA-compliant"
**Confidence**: 5 — the disclosures are consistent across Sections 1.3, 3.1–3.3, 6.4, and Appendices A–C.

### S3: Table 2 does analytic work rather than decorating the text

Table 2 compresses the paper's central distinction into one page: family, enforcement locus, the object claimed "arbitrage-free", guarantee shape, evidence licensed, and verification reported. The note correctly says the coding is the review's rather than a source's claim, which is exactly the qualification the table needs.

**Evidence Anchor**: table: Table 2
**Confidence**: 5 — the table is directly readable against Sections 4.1–4.4 without reconstruction.

### W1: The abstract overgeneralises the certified object to all 41 rows

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "the certified object in all 41 rows is a single date's surface"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction with Table 2 is explicit; the only uncertainty is whether "certified object" is meant loosely enough to cover diagnostic and baseline rows, and the wording does not signal that.

The sentence is defensible for the 31 construction rows, but not for the two supporting groups. Table 2 assigns the conditions/evaluation group the object "Neither constructor nor repair" and the seminal group "No guarantee claim", and Section 4.3 adds that two seminal constructions "did not enforce admissibility at all". The abstract therefore asserts a certified object for rows the body says certify nothing. Because the sentence carries the abstract's static-coding result, the overgeneralisation makes the coding frame look coarser than the body actually is. The Chinese abstract repeats the claim. Scope the sentence to the construction rows, or say "for the 31 construction rows", and state separately that the supporting groups were coded but certify no surface. Both abstract versions need the change.

### W2: The abstract's corpus composition does not add up

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "of which 29 are peer-reviewed and 11 are preprints"
**Confidence**: 5 — the counts are explicit and the missing category is named in Section 3.1 and Table 1.

The abstract gives a denominator of 41 sources and then accounts for 29 peer-reviewed plus 11 preprints, which is 40. The 41st source is the one practitioner magazine article counted in Section 3.1 and Table 1. The omission is small, but the abstract is the surface most readers will quote. Add the magazine article to the breakdown or drop the sub-counts; do not leave a composition that does not sum.

### W3: Production artifacts and unresolved exhibit placeholders remain in the manuscript

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "Figure 1 (source diagram; to be typeset at Phase 7)"
**Confidence**: 5 — the placeholder is literal, appears twice, and sits beside front-matter and appendix references to pipeline stages.

Figures 1 and 2 are ASCII boxes labelled as source diagrams "to be typeset at Phase 7". The front matter carries "Version: v1.3 (Stage 2.5 integrity corrections)", and Appendix A refers to "Stage 1". These are internal pipeline labels, not reader-facing exhibit content, and they signal an untypeset draft. The argument does not depend on the figures — Figure 1 restates Section 2.3 and Figure 2 restates Table 1's arithmetic — so this is a production-readiness defect rather than an analytic one. Replace both exhibits with typeset figures or a table, and remove the stage labels from the front matter and body.

### W4: The VI/VII evidence-level labels are introduced but not explained

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "Evidence-level labels VI (40 rows) and VII (one row)"
**Confidence**: 5 — the labels occur only here, and Appendix C's coded-field list does not mention them.

Section 3.2 says the labels "record the document type and reporting completeness", but it never says what distinguishes level VI from level VII, and the labels are not used in any later count or table. A reader cannot recover the distinction. Define both levels in a clause, or delete the sentence; as written it asks the reader to remember an unexplained instrument.

The D5 defects above are localised: the abstract can be corrected in two sentences, and the production artifacts are mechanical. The body's argument remains fully reconstructible, which is why D5 is assessed at warn rather than block.
