## Review Body

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's central inference is that the four families are not competing answers but four enforcement loci licensing four kinds of evidence, and that their apparent disagreement is a division of evidential labour. The strongest objection is that this inference is close to definitional. Once a method is classified by where a constraint enters — parameter domain, objective, output image, or sampler — the kind of evidence it can offer follows analytically: a domain restriction supports a theorem over an admitted set, a penalty supports a residual at a weight and tolerance, a projection supports a property of a map, and a sampler supports an acceptance rate. The review presents this as an empirical discovery about the literature, but the classification and the evidence label are two descriptions of the same coding decision. A reader can reconstruct the predicted evidence profile from the locus alone, before reading any result.

This matters because the paper uses the framework to dissolve disagreement. If two rows appear to conflict about whether a surface is arbitrage-free, the review says they are certifying different objects, so there is no conflict. That is a redescription, not a reconciliation. The paper never exhibits a case in which the locus classification was genuinely uncertain ex ante and the evidence settled it; the boundary cases R3/R6, N1, and G7/C3 are disclosed as coding choices, not used as tests of the framework. The reader is asked to accept that the four loci are the sites where constraints actually enter, but no argument excludes a fifth site — for example, data cleaning or quote filtering before construction, or a model-calibration layer that imposes consistency across dates. The taxonomy may be exhaustive over the 41 rows, but exhaustiveness over the corpus is not exhaustiveness over the field, and the paper's language occasionally slides between the two.

The non-definitional residue is thinner than the rhetoric. The one paired measurement that bears on the framework's practical claim is G6: a hard-constrained network reports 0.00% violations at $10^{-4}$ over 15 runs on synthetic SVI data, but a higher total-variance RMSE than both the SSVI baseline and the soft-penalty network. The paper reports this against its own thesis, which is to its credit, but one synthetic row cannot establish that stronger guarantees cost fit in general; the result may reflect architecture, training, or the particular synthetic design. The dynamic void is the only field-level finding that is not definitional, and it is the finding most exposed to the corpus construction.

The counter-narrative, then, is this: the paper has produced a useful coding frame and a disciplined corpus description, but its headline inference — that the literature's apparent disagreements are a division of evidential labour — is a restatement of the frame. The paper sometimes concedes this, calling the framework a proposed instrument that is explanatory and untested, but the abstract and §7.1 state the inference as a finding. That gap between a proposed taxonomy and a claimed property of the literature is the core argumentative weakness.

### Alternative Explanations for the Dynamic Void

The paper states two explanations and declines to choose: a genuine gap, and a retrieval or vocabulary effect. Both are plausible, and the refusal to adjudicate is honest. But the menu is incomplete, and the incompleteness matters because the void is called the review's most consequential finding and its most fragile one in §6.2.

A third explanation is selection by the review's own frame. Inclusion requires a paper to address construction, repair, or generation of a surface under an explicit static or dynamic constraint. The exclusion codes then remove 21 forecasting-or-trading records, 4 model-calibration-or-pricing records, 34 out-of-scope-asset records, and 32 publication-type records in §3.1. A joint-calibration or market-model paper that tests process consistency would plausibly be removed by the model-calibration/pricing code or by the forecasting/trading code; a paper on a different asset would be removed by the asset code even if its method is directly relevant. The 40 records excluded as relevant but not selected are discretionary, and the review does not audit them for dynamic tests. If the frame selects against process-level work, the finding is about included static-construction papers, not a void in the literature. The caveat that the absence is not a statement about the field does not repair the framing of a section titled The Dynamic Void.

A fourth explanation is model-level inheritance. §4.4 describes G1, a VAE over stochastic-volatility parameters, as producing surfaces whose admissibility follows from the SDE class rather than from the training objective; G7 is a neural SDE with a static guarantee and an explicit dynamic disclaimer; C3 builds along paths. If an SDE model class carries a martingale measure, dynamic consistency may be inherited structurally even though no path test is reported. The coding rules in §3.2 and Appendix C assign model-class guarantees to proof status, but they do not say whether a model-level guarantee counts as enforcement of the dynamic condition. The 0-of-41 enforcement count therefore rests on an unstated coding decision. The paper needs either a definition of enforcement that requires a stated process-level argument, or a sensitivity check showing which rows would move.

A fifth explanation is abstract-level non-reporting. The corpus was coded from 34 full abstracts, 4 partial abstracts, and 3 titles in §1.3; no full texts were read. A paper may test dynamic consistency in the body and not mention it in the abstract. The hedge that the coding is as reported in the abstracts is accurate, but the abstract's sentence that no source enforces or tests dynamic no-arbitrage travels without that qualifier. This is a reporting-gap explanation, not a field-gap explanation, and it is the most likely to survive a full-text check.

### Scope of the Bounded Absence Claim

The bounded claim is stated carefully in §6.2 and §6.4: no included study, as of the search date, states and tests a process-level dynamic condition, and the absence is bounded to 41 abstracts. The problem is that the most visible statements are not the bounded ones. The abstract says no source enforces or tests dynamic no-arbitrage; §7.1 says dynamic no-arbitrage is neither enforced nor tested anywhere in the corpus and then generalises to the field in the same paragraph. Anywhere in the corpus is defensible; the field is not, and the paper's own retrieval and coding limits make the difference decisive.

The same pattern appears in three other headline claims. First, the certified object in all 41 rows is said to be a single date's surface, but §2.3 and Table 2 record four objects: the admitted parameter set, the evaluated sample, the image of a projection, and the accepted sample or inherited model class. The 41-row total also includes five condition rows and five seminal baselines, which do not construct or repair surfaces in the same sense, so the all-41 formula is not supported by the table. Second, the claim that proof-level and test-level evidence are almost disjoint is supported by the count that only one proof row reports a violation rate, but G6 is both a structural guarantee and an audit, and §5.1 says so. Almost does the work, but the conclusion's universal negative does not carry it. Third, the claim that no two sources state data, metric, and tolerance together is a claim about abstracts, not about the underlying papers. Each of these is repairable by scoping the sentence to the coded corpus.

The bounded absence claim itself is not overgeneralised in §6.2; it is overgeneralised in the places readers will quote. The fix is not new evidence but consistent scope: put the 41 included abstracts qualifier on the abstract sentence and on the §7.1 takeaway, and let the body carry the interpretation.

### Logic-Chain Validation

The chain from evidence to the central inference runs: 41 rows are coded by family and evidence status; each family's evidence type matches its locus; therefore the families are four enforcement loci and their disagreement is a division of labour. The first two steps are descriptive and well documented. The third step is where the argument needs work, because the match between family and evidence type is what the coding scheme was designed to produce. A classification that predicts the label it assigns is not yet evidence that the literature is organised that way. The paper would need either an independent prediction, for example that locus predicts fit cost, stability, or data efficiency, or a case where the framework's prediction failed and was corrected. The G6 result is the closest thing to an independent prediction, and it is one synthetic row.

A second break is the inference from no direct comparison to no ranking is possible in §7.1. Direct is defined by four fields: same dataset, same condition set and tolerance, same metric and aggregation, same protocol. What follows is that no ranking is licensed by the corpus's reported numbers. It does not follow that no ranking is possible. The agenda's second study proposes a pre-registered head-to-head that would produce the first direct comparison in §6.3, which concedes that a common protocol would make ranking possible. The non-ranking stance needs the weaker modal claim.

A third break is the falsification clause for the first agenda study. The paper says a tested dynamic condition already present under a different label would falsify the review's bounded-absence claim in §6.3. A new paper cannot falsify a claim about a frozen 41-row corpus; it can falsify the field-level reading. The paper's own distinction between corpus and field should be applied to its falsification clause.

A fourth is the exhaustiveness of the four loci. §2.3 presents the loci as the sites where constraints enter, and §6.1 maps four meanings of arbitrage-free onto them. No argument rules out a data-level locus, such as filtering or cleaning quotes before construction, or a calibration-level locus that spans dates. The boundary cases are all inside the four loci; none is an outside case. If the taxonomy is meant to be exhaustive, it needs an argument; if it is meant to be a useful partition of this corpus, the language should say so.

### The Non-Ranking Stance

The paper is right that a league table would be misleading: the seven metric families, the different condition sets, the missing tolerances, and the different panels make cross-row comparison unsafe. The discipline of reporting only within-study baselines — G6 against SSVI and the soft-penalty network, N4 against a VAE, N5 against an ANN, and R5 across five backends — is appropriate, and the review does not vote-count. That is a genuine methodological strength.

The stance becomes an overclaim when it moves from the corpus does not license a ranking to no ranking is possible. The paper's own framework is about where constraints enter, not about whether methods can be ordered on cost, stability, or fit once a common protocol exists. The agenda concedes the point by proposing the head-to-head. There is also a smaller inconsistency: the paper uses evaluative comparatives, such as the sharpest evidence profile in §4.1 and the corpus's one clear paired measurement in §4.4, which are clarity judgments rather than method rankings, but they sit awkwardly beside the categorical language. The fix is to distinguish three claims: no ranking is licensed by the reported numbers; a ranking would require a common protocol; and the framework does not itself imply incomparability. The first is supported, the second is supported, and the third is the one that needs saying.

### The "So What?" Test

The review's practical yield is a three-sentence reporting convention covering condition, tolerance, and verification mode, a map of four enforcement loci, and seven proposed studies. The map is useful for readers who need to know what a claim of arbitrage-freeness is worth; the convention is sensible; the agenda is specific. But the paper does not show that any of this changes behaviour or outcomes. There is no evidence that authors would adopt the triple, that reviewers would ask for it, or that venues would enforce it. The one empirical result that bears on method choice, G6's higher fit error under a stronger guarantee, is synthetic and single-row.

The so-what is therefore modest: stop treating arbitrage-free as a single predicate, and report the three fields. That is a real contribution for a review, but it is smaller than the paper's rhetoric, which calls the void the most consequential finding and the family relation a division of evidential labour. The paper would be stronger if it either claimed the modest contribution plainly or supplied a test in which the framework predicts something beyond its own coding, for example that locus predicts the size of the gap between reported and audited violations across rows, or that the reporting triple is absent exactly where guarantees are inherited. As written, the framework explains the corpus after the fact; it does not yet predict anything a sceptical reader could not have predicted from the coding scheme.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

The paper's opening motivates the work with risk numbers, margin, and capital, but the stakeholder analysis never goes beyond a premise. The people who consume surfaces — risk managers, margin desks, central counterparties, regulators — appear as the reason the work matters, not as parties whose needs, constraints, or costs are examined. The people who produce surfaces, the authors of the 41 rows, are asked to adopt a reporting triple whose cost is asserted to be three sentences, with no analysis of who bears that cost, whether it falls unevenly on preprint authors or practitioner outlets, or what incentive they have to comply. The people who are left out — non-English, non-indexed, industry-document, and failed-method work — are disclosed as corpus limits, but the framework's claim to describe the field does not adjust for them.

The premise about benefit is clearest in two places. First, the build-or-buy sentence in §7.2 assumes that a practitioner choosing a method is choosing among enforcement loci, and that the review's cost column is informative enough to support that choice; §5.3 and Table 6 say cost is asserted in about five rows and comparable in none, with interpretability and data efficiency empty. Second, the reporting triple is a cost-shifting proposal: it adds disclosure work for authors and reviewing work for editors, while the benefit is asserted for readers. Neither the incidence nor the magnitude is analysed. These are not prose-level quibbles; they are load-bearing premises for the paper's practical claim, and they are recorded as MAJOR rows below.

The corpus itself also embeds a stakeholder choice. Ten of the twelve rows with known provenance are US indices; the non-US cases are a 2006 DAX study and a 2022 JSE study; 11 rows are preprints and one is a practitioner magazine. The four-locus framework may travel, but the review has not shown that it does, and the voices most likely to enforce constraints differently — exchanges, data vendors, regulators — are absent from the corpus by construction.

### Non-Defect Observations

- The static condition set SC1–SC5 is stated precisely, and the scale-relativity discussion of SABR negative densities versus asymptotically arbitrage-free wings in §2.1 is a model of careful qualification.
- The clarification that calendar arbitrage in the corpus usually means a static maturity-axis condition, not a dynamic condition, in §6.2 is the paper's best conceptual move; it changes how a reader should read several rows.
- The hypothesis adjudication in §5.1 reports disconfirmation rather than forcing support: H1's dynamic clause is disconfirmed as stated, H2's fit-error clause is unsupported, and H3 is split by branch. This is unusually honest.
- G6 is reported against the paper's thesis, including the synthetic-data qualification and the higher error than two baselines.
- The non-ranking discipline is mostly sustained: within-study baselines only, no league table, no vote counting, and explicit metric-family separation.
- The limitations in §6.4 are specific and matched to the method: abstract-level coding, single analyst, English and OpenAlex only, one lost query, no full texts or code, and no recomputation.
- The single-stock exception C5 is disclosed at the point of use and never generalised.
- The ledger, Appendix A, Appendix B, and Appendix C make the corpus auditable; boundary cases R3, R6, N1, G7, C3, and C5 are named rather than smoothed away.
- The AI-use disclosure is candid about the agent's role and the absence of claim-level human verification.
- The prose is clear, the abstract is structured, and the paper generally says what it is doing before it does it.
