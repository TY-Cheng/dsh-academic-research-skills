## Contract Paraphrase

Methodology rigour concerns whether the research design, the data handling, the statistical reporting and the affordances for reproduction meet the peer-review bar of this field. Read from where I sit, the point is not generic tidiness but whether the procedures actually reported are the procedures this subfield uses for this class of study, and whether the estimation chain is surfaced far enough that another specialist can see which modelling assumptions produced the headline numbers.

Domain accuracy is the dimension I own. It asks whether the manuscript's claims agree with current evidence in the field, whether prior work is represented in a way its own authors would recognise, and whether field-specific terminology and reported results are factually correct. For me the practical test is whether the paper's definitions, attributions and stated results can be checked line by line against the canonical statements of the field it invokes, and whether a specialist reading only those statements would arrive at the same claim the manuscript does.

Argumentative coherence concerns whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually bears on the claims, and whether any premise the argument depends on is left unsupported, including premises about who benefits, who bears the cost, or who is left out. From the domain side, I check the substantive premises: a logically tidy chain still fails if a premise about how the market or the literature works is false, because coherence is not a substitute for correct field content.

Cross-disciplinary relevance asks whether the framing, definitions and implications are accessible to readers from adjacent fields and whether any interdisciplinary claim is substantiated rather than gestured at. A domain specialist's share of this is whether field-specific constructs are introduced before they are relied on, whether borrowed vocabulary is used in the sense the lending discipline gives it, and whether claims that reach into another discipline are attributed to that literature.

Writing and structure covers organisation, clarity of exposition, exhibit quality and adherence to venue conventions. My domain-facing concern is narrower: whether nomenclature is used consistently, whether exhibits carry the units, conventions and labels the field expects, and whether a reader trained in this area can reconstruct what each exhibit shows without a trip to the appendix.

Venue fit and contribution concerns whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it adds something original and significant for that readership. My contribution-side question is domain-specific: whether the advance is a genuine increment in what the field knows, correctly distinguished from what earlier work already established, rather than a restatement of known results under new labels.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: whether the manuscript's definitions, attributions and stated results match the field's canonical statements; whether each cited source is represented as its own authors would recognise; whether sufficiency, necessity, scope and boundary-case claims carry the right logical status; and whether the stated contribution is positioned against the literature a specialist would expect
what_triggers_block: one or more substantive domain errors that a specialist would treat as disqualifying, such as a misattributed canonical result, an inverted definition, or a characterisation of prior work that its authors would reject, with the error reaching a claim the manuscript relies on
what_triggers_warn: domain imprecision or incomplete attribution that a specialist would flag and expect corrected in revision, without any single error overturning a claim the manuscript relies on
what_triggers_fatal: the manuscript's central contribution depends on a domain proposition that is false in the cited literature and cannot be repaired without withdrawing the contribution itself, so the defect survives any amount of rewriting
